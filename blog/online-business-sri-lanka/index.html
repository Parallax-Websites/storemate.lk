Online Business in Sri Lanka: How to Start and Run One (Beginner's Guide)

Storemate· November 12, 2026· 5 min read

Starting a business online in Sri Lanka no longer needs a shop, a large budget or a technical background. Many sellers begin with a Facebook page, a phone and a handful of products.

The harder part comes later: keeping orders, payments and deliveries organized as customers grow. This guide walks through how to start and run an online business in Sri Lanka, step by step.

Step 1: Choose What to Sell

Start with a product you can source reliably and explain clearly. Good starting points are things you already know well, products with steady local demand, or items you can get at a price that leaves room for delivery costs.

Test with a small batch before committing to large stock.

02

Step 2: Decide Where to Sell

Most Sri Lankan online sellers use one or more of these:

  • Facebook and Instagram pages, where customers message to order
  • WhatsApp, for repeat customers and quick confirmations
  • Your own website, which gives you control over your brand
  • Online marketplaces, which bring existing shoppers but come with their own rules and fees

Starting with one channel is easier. Add more once the first one runs smoothly.

03

Step 3: Set Up How You Get Paid

Cash on delivery is still a very common way for Sri Lankan customers to pay online, and many sellers also accept bank transfers. Decide which methods you'll offer and make them clear to customers before they order.

If you accept COD, plan for the fact that some orders will be refused at the door, and price that risk into your business.

04

Step 4: Plan Your Delivery

Delivery is where online businesses win or lose customers. Compare couriers on price, coverage and reliability for the areas you deliver to.

Many sellers end up using more than one courier, for example one for Colombo and another for outstation areas. Be honest with customers about delivery times.

05

Step 5: Manage Your Orders

With a few orders a day, a notebook or spreadsheet works. As volume grows, things start to slip: an order is missed, a customer's address is mistyped, or you can't tell which COD payments have come back. This is the point where many sellers move to order management software.

Storemate is built for online sellers in Sri Lanka to manage orders and sync couriers in one place, so you aren't switching between courier portals to dispatch and follow up each order. It integrates with five major Sri Lankan couriers: Domex, Royal Express, Koombiyo, TransExpress and FDE.

06

Step 6: Look After Your Customers

Reply quickly, confirm orders before dispatch, and keep customers updated on delivery. Handle returns and complaints politely.

In a small business, a good reputation spreads by word of mouth, and a bad one spreads just as fast.

07

Step 7: Check the Legal and Tax Basics

Registration and tax requirements for businesses in Sri Lanka can change, and they depend on your business type and size. Check the current rules with the relevant authorities, such as the Inland Revenue Department for tax, or ask a local accountant, before you grow.

08

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Selling on too many channels before the first one is under control
  • Not tracking COD payments, so money goes missing between couriers and your account
  • Promising delivery times you can't keep
  • Keeping everything in chat threads and memory instead of one organized system

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start an online business in Sri Lanka?

It depends on what you sell. Many sellers start small, with a modest first batch of stock and a free social media page, and reinvest as sales grow.

Do I need a website to sell online?

No. Many sellers start on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. A website helps later, when you want more control over your brand.

Is cash on delivery a good option for a new business?

It builds customer trust, but it also brings refused orders and slower payment. Track it carefully from the start.

When should I start using order management software?

When you're taking orders from more than one place, using more than one courier, or spending a noticeable part of your day copying order details between apps.

Conclusion

An online business in Sri Lanka is easy to start and harder to run well. Begin small, choose one sales channel, get your payments and deliveries sorted, and organize your orders before they become a problem.

The sellers who grow steadily are usually the ones who build good habits early.

Put your first orders, deliveries and COD payments in one system now, instead of untangling chat threads later.
